The most common barrier to government UAS capability is not technology. It is procurement architecture.

An agency that identifies a requirement for NDAA-compliant platforms faces a standard acquisition process: market research, source selection, award, delivery, training, and initial operational capability. Under a standard capital acquisition model, that timeline routinely runs six to eighteen months from requirement identification to first operational flight. For requirements driven by operational urgency, that timeline is not useful.

Two service models address this directly: Drones as a Service and Procurement as a Service. Both exist today in the Forge and Flight enterprise. Neither requires a capital acquisition cycle.

## Drones as a Service: Forge Flex Mission Platform as a Service

The Forge Flex program is Forge and Flight's Drones as a Service offering — structured as a Mission Platform as a Service (MPaaS) retainer that provides NDAA-compliant platform access, parts supply, technical support, and training through a recurring subscription rather than a capital purchase.

A unit or agency on Forge Flex retains access to the platform family it needs for mission execution without owning the asset, carrying the inventory burden, or managing the parts supply chain. Forge Flex covers:

**Platform access and configuration support.** Subscribers access the platform tier appropriate for their mission profile — from the Group 1 [Longbow-8](/solutions/) through Group 3 [Titan-20](/solutions/) configurations. Platform configuration, payload integration, and mission setup support are included.

**Parts supply and consumables.** Attrition is expected in operational programs. Forge Flex subscribers receive priority access to replacement motors, ESCs, propellers, and structural components from NDAA-compliant domestic supply chains, with guaranteed delivery windows that do not depend on open-market availability.

**Technical support.** Remote diagnostics, configuration troubleshooting, and field guidance are included at every tier. Subscribers do not wait on contractor scheduling — support is accessible when the platform is grounded.

**Training integration.** Forge Flex tiers include training credits applicable to the [Forge and Flight Academy](/forge-flex/) course catalog. Organizations can apply retainer credits toward operator qualification, advanced sustainment training, and instructor certification.

Forge Flex is available through standard government contracting vehicles including GPC, MIPR, and simplified acquisition. Contracting entity: Forge and Flight Holdings, Inc. (CAGE 18WR3, SAM Active). Response time for proposals: 48 hours.

This is what Drones as a Service looks like in a defense and government context. No capital purchase. No 12-month procurement cycle. No single-vendor lock-in on parts. Operational capability at the pace of a service agreement, not a major acquisition.

## Procurement as a Service: Federal Services Division

Not every agency requirement is met by Forge and Flight's own manufactured platforms. Some requirements call for systems beyond the Group 1 through 3 range. Some require platforms that are already on a program-of-record or contract vehicle. Some require specialized sensors or payloads that come from other manufacturers.

The Federal Services Division is Forge and Flight's Procurement as a Service capability — a domestic small business with three active CAGE codes, SAM.gov registrations, and an NDAA-compliant supply chain infrastructure that can source, validate, and deliver third-party NDAA-compliant UAS systems to federal customers as a prime or subcontract vehicle.

Agencies working with the Federal Services Division do not navigate an unfamiliar vendor landscape to find compliant systems. They work with a registered federal contractor that has already done the compliance verification, knows the supply chain, and can respond to RFQs with documentation in days rather than weeks.

This is Procurement as a Service in practice: an agency with a UAS requirement contacts the Federal Services Division, receives a compliant source with verified documentation, and awards a contract to a domestic small business that holds its own CAGE code, SAM registration, and NDAA compliance posture.

The Federal Services Division responds to RFQs, sources NDAA-compliant platforms from verified domestic suppliers, and delivers to federal customers under simplified acquisition thresholds and above. CAGE 18WR3. SAM Active. SPRS 110/110.

## Which Model Fits Which Requirement

**Forge Flex (Drones as a Service)** is the right model for units and agencies that need recurring access to NDAA-compliant platforms, want to avoid capital purchase overhead, need to scale platform access up or down with mission tempo, and prefer a subscription vehicle to a capital acquisition line.

**Federal Services Division (Procurement as a Service)** is the right model for units and agencies with a specific platform requirement that may not be met by Forge and Flight's own manufactured lineup, need a domestic small business prime with sourcing and compliance verification infrastructure, or require a vendor that can respond to RFQs and deliver under simplified acquisition thresholds.

Both models are available now. Both are available through standard government contracting vehicles. Neither requires a six-month acquisition cycle to access.
