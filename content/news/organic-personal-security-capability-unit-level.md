There is a pattern in how DoD units approach skills that matter but are not directly tied to the primary mission: they contract it. Someone comes in, delivers a training event, and leaves. The unit has certificates. The unit does not have capability.

Personal digital security is in that pattern right now. Most units that care about it bring in a contractor for an annual security awareness event. Operators get a briefing. They might get a checklist. The contractor leaves. Six months later, the threat environment has changed, new personnel have rotated in, and the unit's actual security posture is roughly what it was before the contractor arrived, because the knowledge left with the contractor.

Organic capability looks different.

## What Organic Actually Means

An organic capability is one that lives in the unit and does not require external resources to sustain. For personal digital security, that means at minimum: personnel who can harden their own devices, configure and maintain a working communications security posture, reduce their personal data-broker exposure, and adapt their practices when the threat environment changes.

A unit with this capability does not need to wait for the next annual training event when a new threat technique emerges. The personnel who understand why each security decision was made can evaluate a new threat and adapt. Personnel who received a checklist cannot.

[FFP-201 at Forge and Flight Academy](/courses/ffp-201/) is built to produce this second type of operator. The five-day course is structured around a threat model each student builds on Day 1. Every tool, every configuration, every identity decision through the rest of the week is justified by what that threat model produced. Graduates understand the reasoning, not just the steps.

## The Multiplication Effect

The organic capability argument gets stronger when you consider what FFP-201 graduates can do for others in the unit.

An operator who has built and operated a live PACE plan can help the next rotation set one up. Someone who has reduced their data-broker exposure once can do it again and teach others to do it.
